UX Designer · May 2018 – March 2020

CareCloud

This was my first UX role, right after five years as a graphic designer — and I was lucky to join at the start of something ambitious: a full rebuild of a legacy SaaS product from the ground up.

Analytics dashboards — one of the seven core sections of the rebuild.

I started by owning the guarantor center — essentially a patient record, but for the financial and administrative side of care. By the end of my time there, I'd grown into five of the seven core sections: claim remittance, front office, settings, patient portals, and the guarantor center, alongside working with our UI Engineering Lead on a shared component library.

Most days meant sitting with internal stakeholders and multi-million dollar clients to understand where our designs fell short of their actual workflows. At one point, we shadowed users working side by side in a competitor's product just to see the gap for ourselves.

I worked closely with our Head of Product, project managers, and engineering leads — and by the latter half of my time there, I was interviewing, onboarding, and managing timelines for two designers who joined the team.

It was a fast pace — our small team of two produced over 3,000 mocks in a single year — but it also gave me some of the most genuinely complex problems I've worked on.

The scheduler nobody thought could work

The one I'm proudest of is the scheduler, part of the front office workflow. Clinician schedules aren't simple — hard rules on overbooking, custom logic for which visit types could happen at which hours, multiple locations to track, and front office staff who needed to find availability fast, often mid-call with a patient who needed more than one clinician in the room.

There were so many intersecting rules that a lot of people doubted we could make it feel calm instead of chaotic. We did. I still remember the high of watching something that complicated finally click into place for the people using it every day.

Product Designer, Brand Lead · March 2020 – November 2021

Restaurant Brands
International

I joined RBI the day the world shut down.

Three global brands — Burger King, Popeyes, and Tim Hortons — with over 100 million user sessions a month between them, and almost overnight, ordering through an app stopped being a convenience and became the only way a lot of people could get food safely.

Fast food had never felt essential before. Suddenly, it was.

Mocks and prototypes across all three brands, plus the offer CMS behind them.

I started by building high-fidelity mocks and prototypes across all three brands, then grew into leading brand-agnostic authentication, acting as design lead and liaison for the Tim Hortons brand, and owning Burger King's international markets. I also got to spearhead a micro-brand's MVP from first idea through to engineering implementation.

Beyond the day-to-day design work, I supported other designers on the team and led initiatives in our CMS and franchisee portal, working closely with our engineering lead to design around the real technical limitations of point-of-sale hardware — a constraint healthcare software never had to teach me.

Product Designer · November 2021 – April 2023

Optimize Health

After nearly two years away from healthcare, coming back to Optimize felt like a breath of fresh air. I'd learned a lot in the QSR world, but complex, high-stakes problems were where I actually wanted to be.

The patient list — the spine of the product we rebuilt.

We started at ground zero. The working POC that users were currently using was no longer cutting it — it was time to grow into the product people were envisioning. Within a year, my fellow designer and I had built out a complex patient record and communication tool, organization management, tasking, and a full billing reporting solution — essentially, the product, from the ground up. Alongside the high-fidelity mocks and prototypes, we ran research through Userlytics and kept a regular cadence of sessions with internal users, our CS team, and clients themselves.

I also built and managed an in-depth Figma component library — not a coded one, but a system that made sure the patterns we designed stayed consistent and carried through cleanly to code. We'd occasionally direct a third-party design firm when we needed extra hands, giving them the documentation to work within our existing patterns.

Designing every state, not just the happy one

Patient search had to hold up when a name returned nothing, returned too much, or returned one exact match mid-call. Four states, one pattern.

Mentorship

Around the same time I joined, so did a designer just making the shift into UX — her background was in user analytics. Over the two years we worked together, I mentored her through everything from design patterns and process to component creation and problem solving, with a real focus on strengthening her relationship with engineering: understanding their constraints and requests, and finding solutions that worked for everyone.

Director of Product Design · May 2023 – Present

Medbridge

It isn't just about designing. At Medbridge, my work has centered on shaping product vision and driving alignment — not just defining end states, but helping teams see possibilities early, build buy-in, and make confident bets in moments of uncertainty.

I've helped guide the evolution from a primarily education-focused platform into a scalable care ecosystem, supporting a rebrand, launching a new care platform, integrating more care experiences, and growing the design function from a team of one to three. I see design not just as execution, but as a way to shape direction, momentum, and belief.

Chapter two

Building the team I wanted to lead

During my time at Medbridge, I've interviewed 30+ designers while learning that building a strong team takes more than finding talented people. I had to learn which skills, perspectives, and personalities complemented each other—and which qualities mattered far more in practice than they did on a résumé.

But hiring was only the beginning. I learned that leading a team wasn't just about making sure the work was good. It was about creating an environment where people could grow: giving designers room to stretch, helping them find goals they were genuinely excited about, and creating opportunities to learn from one another.

Eventually, I realized my job wasn't to build a team that could execute my vision. It was to build one where each designer could develop their own.

Chapter three

Rethinking what the role looks like

For the last nine months, my team and I have been asking what "design engineer" actually means, and shifting how we work to match. We fully coded our Storybook component library from the ground up — not just designed it, built it — and started shipping front-end code alongside engineering, closing the communication gap that handoff so often creates. I also fully built an internal tool myself, one that lets my team see the health of our core workflows, set goals against them, and track how shipped work actually moves the needle.
